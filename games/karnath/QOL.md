# The Staff of Karnath: changes from the original

This is a translated port of the 1984 Commodore 64 game: every routine of the original translated to TypeScript and checked
against the original running on an emulated C64 (`re/README.md`). **ORIGINAL or ENHANCED:** one switch, E on the pause
screen (on the right; tap it on a phone); ENHANCED is the default and turns on the rows marked ENHANCED, quality-of-life
changes for a modern playthrough. ORIGINAL is the game as it was. Rows marked Always are in both modes.

| Feature | The original | In this port | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pause screen and map** | F7 paused the game. | P, Esc, pad Start or the gear: the game waits while a pause screen shows a map of the whole castle as the game keeps it: its levels one under another (the upper floor's three, the lower floor's three, the dungeons, Karn's abode), each a strip at its real length, lit where you've walked, the rooms marked where you came into them (pick one and its name shows), and the stairs and ways between floors you've taken; where you are blinking, energy and progress, and the options (O: a page of every option and assist with its key, to tap or press). | Always |
| **Travel back** | None: every step on foot. | On the pause screen: the arrows pick any room he's been in, Enter takes him back to where he first came into it (across levels, between the castle's halves, into the dungeons and Karn's abode), the way the game's own changes of level do; nothing taken or opened comes back. He is placed along the strip as walking in places him, so the creatures the level starts (the guard room's knights, the dungeons' skull...) are on the screen as when walked in to. | Always |
| **Back to room start** | A trap with no way out means starting again. | B on the pause screen: back to how things were on coming into this room (the latest autosave). | Always |
| **Back to the title** | None (only by losing, or a restart). | T on the pause screen (or a tap on it): the title, with the save kept; ENTER CONTINUE goes back to it, and only a new game started there begins a new one (keeping the save as the previous one). A new game over a save asks first: NEW GAME? (Space or fire again yes, Esc or a tap no, Enter continues); a jump or fire still held as the last life goes brings CONTINUE? up instead of starting one. | Always |
| **Autosave** | None. | Progress is saved in your browser each time Pendragon comes onto a new screen. Launched with a save, the game opens on its title with ENTER CONTINUE, and Enter goes back into play where it was (T on the pause screen goes back to the title the same way). Starting a new game keeps the save from before it as the previous one: the saved games (F1 on the directory, or the gear there) bring it back, or delete a save (they ask first). | Always |
| **Continue** | When his energy runs out, back to the title. | "CONTINUE?" (Enter yes, Esc no; it waits for an answer): back to the screen he last came onto, with full energy. Not offered after F1 (restart on purpose). | Always |
| **Action keys** | One fire button for the current action; any other key cycles through the actions, but only once it has been held down for 16 frames in all (the frames of quick taps add up), so a tap took five or six presses. | 1-9 and 0 choose one of the ten spells directly; Space casts; Enter, the touch SPELL button or any other key moves on to the next spell, one press one spell, at any moment (with a spell in flight too). | Always |
| **Combat effects** | A creature the right spell reaches is put somewhere else at once (a new one), or turned; a hurt flashes Pendragon and takes energy. | Drawn over the picture, never in it, from the moments in the game's own code (re/README.md, "Combat"): an enemy destroyed by a spell flashes white (a pale one yellow), a ring goes out and it bursts into pixels of its own colours, thrown away from the blow; a blow that doesn't destroy sparks; the spell leaves a fading trail; a hurt flashes the picture's edges red, shakes it a pixel or two and pulses the energy left in the status line. Each has a sound of its own, made as a C64 game makes them, on a second SID (the game's own sound untouched). | Always |
| **Running** | Pendragon walks. | Hold Shift to run: his own movement runs twice a frame (enemies, air and timers keep their pace). | When Shift is held |
| **Smooth movement** | Locked to a 50 Hz PAL television; sprites the game multiplexes flicker. | Game logic still runs at the original 50 Hz; sprites glide sideways between frames on 60/120/144 Hz screens, and every sprite shows every frame. Up and down they are drawn where the game puts them, so a figure stands on its floor. | Always |
| **Title screen keys** | The title lists the C64's keys (F7, F1, "any other key", the joystick button). | Rewritten for this keyboard: P to pause, R to restart, Enter to change, Space or fire to start (same layout, the game's own font). | Always |
| **Controls** | Joystick in port 2; F7 pauses, F1 restarts, any other key changes the action. | Arrows/WASD, Space (or Z, X, pad A) for fire, P or Esc for the pause screen, R (or F1) to restart, Enter (or any other key) changes the action; gamepads. M mutes. | Always |
| **Touch controls** (phones, tablets) | None. | 8-way stick, CAST, SPELL (the next spell), RUN (on/off); PAUSE opens the map (tap a place, tap again to go; tap an option to change it); RETURN key, HOME. Landscape with the controls beside the picture (even with rotation locked), or upright. See docs/mobile.md. | On a touch screen |
| **A moment's grace** | None. | For 3 seconds (150 frames) after the game puts you back into play, no hurt starts (the game's own hurt is cancelled where it begins, as SAFE AFTER A HIT does): continuing a save (ENTER CONTINUE, CONTINUE? after losing), back to room start, arriving from the map. Quality of life for a modern playthrough, so a resume never starts in the middle of a fight; no sign on screen. Both modes. | Always |
| **ENHANCED: safe after a hit** | Each hurt starts as soon as the last one's flash ends: a creature still touching him after a hurt's flash hurts him again. | For 1.5 seconds after a hurt starts, no new one does (the game's own start of it is stopped). | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: knockback** | What touches him stays on him. | When hurt he's pushed away from what hit him: his own walk for 6 frames, three steps a frame (walls stop him as ever; not at the screen's edge). | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: hit-stop** | None. | The game waits 4 frames when an enemy is destroyed (the effects go on), so the blow lands. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: 8-way controls** | The joystick's diagonals are each one of the four ways: up+right goes up, up+left left, down+right right, down+left down. Fire strikes the way he faces, so to strike behind him he walks that way first. | A diagonal moves both ways at once: sideways as ever, and his own step into or out of the picture every other frame (his own pace; walls stop it as they stop him). At a door, stairs or a corner that way he goes straight there, through as walking straight does, then on diagonally. A jump goes the same way. Fire with a direction turns him that way first, then strikes (a diagonal sideways). Keyboard: two arrows (or WASD) at once; touch: the stick's diagonals; pads. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: hints** | Nothing says where a piece of the pentacle goes, how the game is won, or that midnight loses it (the game's status line only says PIECE OF PENTACLE FOUND, CARRYING PIECE OF PENTACLE). | Sparkles on the pieces still to take in the room (the map's and the ones lying as sprites; none while one is carried), while one is carried on the way down (the $CF into the dungeons, the DOOR signs on the floor's front edge), on the dungeons' far door, and in Karn's abode on the obelisk once it is on the screen; after the sixteenth, on the staff. And Pendragon says on the game's own status line (its lettering, in the place of its message, never over it): on the first piece taken where it goes (KARNS ABODE, below the dungeons' far door, the obelisk at the far left, BARACATA, key 7), on later ones a short reminder, the count of pieces laid (N OF 16), after the sixteenth the staff (cast MENHIR, key 5; any other spell is death), and one warning at 11 PM. The pause screen counts the pieces laid (PIECES LAID n OF 16, and one carried). All from the game's own memory; nothing the game does is touched (`re/README.md`, "Hints"). | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: the ending** | The staff of Karnath is destroyed, the game says so ("THE STAFF OF KARNATH HAS BEEN DESTROYED... CONGRATULATIONS") and goes back to the title. | A reward for finishing, after the game's own ending (which is as it was): a montage of the castle's rooms, each the game itself running on a new game of its own with its creatures moving as they do and Pendragon running through it by the joystick, doing one thing that fits (FORTHRIN at the knights and the ghost, the great hall run through, a piece laid at the obelisk with BARACATA, the sixteenth and the staff in its shrine with MENHIR in hand, stopped short of the spell that destroys it), each place named by the game itself above its message; credits typed over the montage into the message line in the game's own lettering (the game and its makers, facts about it, this port: a line at a time; sources in `re/credits.md`), since the picture has no empty band to put them in, the castle's creatures walking across the rooms Pendragon runs through (places they are never in), their own pictures and frames at their own size, each named for a moment in the same line as it passes the middle (ours: the game names none), so every one has been seen by the end; then THE END with the pieces laid, rooms seen, energy left and the castle's clock, and back to the title (about 70 seconds in all). The tune is the one the castle plays (the title is silent); any key or tap skips it after two seconds. Winning is remembered (apart from the saves, so a new game never loses it): from then on W on the pause screen shows it again, and `?ending` on the page shows it at once. Quality of life for a modern playthrough: a finish worth watching. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: Dragonskulle's figure** | Pendragon is the blond wizard in blue. | Pendragon is drawn as he is in Dragonskulle (his face, green tunic and black cape), in every pose: standing, each walk step, and casting (the arm out). His feet stand where the wizard's did, and what touches what is the game's own (a picture only). His death, as the game draws it, is unchanged. | ENHANCED |

## Not changed

Everything else is the original: every room, every enemy, the puzzles, the
timings and the sound. All ten spells look the same in the air (one sprite; what differs is what each works on), the dungeons'
red skull is not hurt by any spell, and the game is won by taking sixteen pieces of the pentacle, one at a time, to the obelisk at
the far left of Karn's abode, casting BARACATA at it for each, then MENHIR at the staff (`re/README.md`, "How the game is won").

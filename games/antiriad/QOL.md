# The Sacred Armour of Antiriad: changes from the original

This is the 1986 Commodore 64 game as it was, a native port from the original's own code and data (checked frame by frame
against the original running; `re/README.md`). **ORIGINAL or ENHANCED:** one switch, E on the pause screen; ENHANCED is the
default and turns on the rows marked ENHANCED, quality-of-life changes for a modern playthrough. ORIGINAL is the game as it
was. Rows marked Always are in both modes.

| Feature | The original | In this port | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Title** | The arch (Tal finds the armour and flies off in it) and the credits page, in turn, with their tunes; "PRESS FIRE TO PLAY". | The same sequence, ported; the credits page says SPACE OR FIRE TO PLAY. | Always |
| **Key words** | The end messages say "PRESS FIRE TO PLAY AGAIN". | They say PRESS SPACE OR FIRE TO PLAY AGAIN. | Always |
| **Pause screen** (P, Esc, pad Start, the gear) | No pause. | The shared runstop64 pause screen: a map of the 69 rooms on C64-Wiki's grid (columns A-O, levels 1-14; the power centre is H1), rooms appearing as Tal visits them, where the armour stands, the radiation levels; parts found, life and the armour's energy. | Always |
| **Travel** | None. | From the map, go back to a room Tal has been in (in the armour, rooms the armour has been in): out of the armour he arrives as through a teleporter, fading in where he first came in; in it, the armour arrives where it first came in, flying, as it comes into a room by an edge (standing, it takes off first: that place is often in the air). Rooms he can't go back to as he is now (in the armour, the rooms it has never been in; out of it, those he has only flown through) are drawn dark and can't be picked: a spot Tal came in by on foot can be a gap or a ledge the armour doesn't fit, and the game's own ways of bringing the armour into a room are its edges only. While Tal is dying, lying dead or coming back to life, and once the game is over, every room is dark: travel would cut his death short (the game's own teleporter takes him only standing). In the armour into the last two rooms it's as by their edge: without the Particle Negator the armour's energy goes. The room takes as long to draw as always. (`test/travel.ts`, `test/centre.ts`) | Always |
| **Autosave and Continue** | Five rejuvenations, then "YOU HAVE DIED", back to the title. | Progress is saved in the browser each time Tal comes into a room. The title offers Continue (ENTER); after the game is over, "CONTINUE?" goes back to the last room entered, as it was then, with full life and all the rejuvenations. Launched with a save, the game goes straight back into play where it was (the title only without one, or with T on the pause screen). Starting a new game keeps the save from before it as the previous one: the saved games (F1 on the directory, or the gear there) bring it back, or delete a save (they ask first). A save of a game that went on past its game over (Tal out of his last death, "YOU HAVE DIED" going round, nothing to end the game) goes on from there on loading as CONTINUE? would have, with full life and all the rejuvenations. After the end (the original: "PRESS FIRE TO PLAY AGAIN", fire a new game at once) fire goes to the title, which offers ENTER CONTINUE (SPACE a new game, the finished one kept as the previous save) into New Game+ (below). | Always |
| **Back to the title** | None (only by losing, or a restart). | T on the pause screen (or a tap on it): the title, with the save kept; ENTER CONTINUE goes back to it, and only a new game started there begins a new one (keeping the save as the previous one). A new game over a save asks first: NEW GAME? (Space or fire again yes, Esc or a tap no, Enter continues); a jump or fire still held as the last life goes brings CONTINUE? up instead of starting one. | Always |
| **New Game+** | None: after the ending, a new game. | After the ending, ENTER CONTINUE starts New Game+ on the first screen, in the armour, with all four parts fitted: its energy never runs down, there's no radiation anywhere (out of the armour it never hurts Tal, and the interlock lets him out in any room), and the armour can travel to any room he has been in, on foot or in it. The map and what's been found carry over. The panel's scroller announces it on starting, and its message window shows NEW GAME+ whenever no other message is going by (the pause screen and the saved games say so too). Landing on the pedestal with the Implosion Mine plays the ending again, and ENTER CONTINUE then starts New Game+ again. A save made as the mine went off loads as New Game+. (`test/won.ts`) | Always |
| **Restart** | Q (during play). | R or F1. | Always |
| **Smooth movement** | Locked to a 50 Hz PAL television. | Game logic runs one of the original's frames per 50 Hz tick; sprites glide between ticks on 60/120/144 Hz screens. V switches to whole-pixel steps. | Smooth |
| **Sound** | Richard Joseph's music and effects. | The same, played by the game's own music driver. M turns sound off and on. | On |
| **Controls** | Joystick (port 2). | Arrows/WASD, gamepads, touch (an 8-way stick, FIRE and JUMP). In ORIGINAL, Space, Z, X and both touch buttons are the original's one fire button. | Always |
| **A moment's grace** | None. | For 3 seconds (150 frames) after the game puts you back into play, nothing hurts Tal or the armour, and the radiation and the armour's drain wait: continuing a save (ENTER CONTINUE, a launch going straight back into play, CONTINUE? after losing), back to room start, arriving from the map. Quality of life for a modern playthrough, so a resume never starts in the middle of a fight; no sign on screen. Both modes. | Always |
| **Hits and kills you feel** | A creature destroyed just explodes in its own frames; a hurt is the life or energy bar going down. | The shared combat effects the Pendragon ports have (EFFECTS, on in both modes: presentation over the picture, the game itself untouched): a creature destroyed by a stone, the Pulsar Beam or a touch flashes and bursts into its own pixels with a kill sound; Tal or the armour hurt flashes the screen's edges red with a small shake and a hurt sound. Nothing during a moment's grace, nor for the radiation's slow drain. (`test/feel.ts`) | Always |
| **ENHANCED: Fire and jump apart** | One button: standing still it throws a stone, walking it jumps (so you can't throw on the move or jump without walking first); up jumps only from standing, straight up. | FIRE (Space, X, Ctrl, pad buttons, touch FIRE) only attacks: a stone (walking, he stops and makes the original's standing throw, frame for frame; while his last stone is still flying there's no throw, as standing, and he walks on) or, in the armour, the Pulsar Beam. JUMP (Z, touch JUMP, or up) jumps from walking or standing, the way the stick is pushed (or straight up): the game's own jumps, frame for frame (`test/controls.ts`); a tap between the joystick's reads (every 7th frame) still counts. In the armour JUMP lifts it, as up does. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: An empty armour lets Tal out** | Out of energy ("SUIT INOPERATIVE RECHARGE CELL DEPLETED") the armour drops and bounces on the floor for good, steering dead: it only lands, so Tal can step out, if held the other way from the way it flew until it slows, turns to face out and lands. Let go or pushed the way it flew, it never does. In the first ten rooms (the power centre's, radiation 3) the safety interlock never lets him out, and nothing in the armour can hurt him, so the game never ends: a dead end. | Out of energy it sets down on the floor it comes to, whichever way it faces, and left or right lets Tal out, anywhere (an empty armour's interlock doesn't hold him; outside, the radiation is his to face), so he can fetch a recharge cell (crouch to pick one up) and come back: stepping in with it fills the armour, as in the original. The pause screen says how under ARMOUR 0 (STEP OUT: LEFT/RIGHT; in ORIGINAL, STEP OUT: BACKWARDS, or NO WAY OUT IN ORIGINAL in the first ten rooms). (`test/empty.ts`, with ORIGINAL checked against the original running.) | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: Recharge cells kept** | Tal carries at most one recharge cell ("ALREADY CARRYING RECHARGE CELL" for a second, which stays where it is), used up the moment he steps into the armour, whether it needed it or not. | Tal keeps every recharge cell he crouches over (each once, as the game marks a room's cell taken) and the count is saved with the game. C (the touch CELL button) in the armour fills it from one, when its energy isn't full; stepping into the armour with a cell and an armour that isn't full does too, as the original does. The panel says RECHARGE CELL STORED and the pause screen's side panel shows CELLS and the count (and, under an empty ARMOUR 0, C: REFUEL). So an armour that runs out of energy is never the end of a run once Tal has a cell to hand. ORIGINAL: one cell, as it was. (`test/cells.ts`) | ENHANCED |

## Not changed

Everything else is the original's: all 69 rooms (identical to the C64's, byte for byte), their
animated characters, the radiation, Tal's walking, jumping, falling and throwing, the armour's
flight, bumps and hovering, the parts of the armour and the recharge cells where the original
has them, every creature's movement script, the dissolving and materialising, the teleporters,
the panel (the scrolling messages, the rolling score, the energy bars, the power-up), the
power centre and the ending; even how long a room takes to draw (the playfield dark, nothing
moving), which the port matches to the original's own timing.

The power centre's ending is the original's too: with the Implosion Mine the armour must land on the
top of the pedestal under the dome, and that top flashes through seven colours, a frame each, of which
only two are floor; the armour lands on it only if it reaches it in one of those, otherwise it sinks
into it and stands a row lower, on the pedestal's body, where the game doesn't look for the power
centre: nothing happens, and it takes off and comes down again (about half of all landings work, in the
original as here: `test/centre.ts`).

The original's oddities are kept: room J13's way down leads to a room that doesn't exist (room
$45), the radiation warning's timer is sometimes taken from the wrong level, and a stone or beam
that hits a wall leaves its movement script set to a stray value (all harmless in play).

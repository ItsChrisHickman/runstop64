# Blackwyche: changes from the original

This is a translated port of the 1985 Commodore 64 game: every routine of the original translated to TypeScript and checked
against the original running on an emulated C64 (`re/README.md`). **ORIGINAL or ENHANCED:** one switch, E on the pause
screen (on the right; tap it on a phone); ENHANCED is the default and turns on the rows marked ENHANCED, quality-of-life
changes for a modern playthrough. ORIGINAL is the game as it was. Rows marked Always are in both modes.

| Feature | The original | In this port | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pause screen and map** | F7 paused the game. | P, Esc, pad Start or the gear: the game waits while a pause screen shows a map of the whole galleon, laid out from the game's own data: its five decks one under another, each going round as the game keeps it (a hall, the deck's long first part, the hall at its other end, the long second part), the cabins over their doors and the ladders and trapdoors between them; dark grey until you've been there, lit where you have, places you can't travel to drawn dark (NO TRAVEL); where you are blinking, energy and progress (ITEMS: how many of the 24 things to find he has; SEEN: how much of the ship), and the options (O: a page of every option and assist with its key, to tap or press). A second page (the pause screen's page keys, < > or a tap on the heading's ends) lists the things he has collected, in the game's own names: white taken, green where the game has used it (the mirror, the water), dark not yet. The game itself only names the last thing found. The places have names (ours: the game names none, src/names.ts), shown for the place you're in and the one picked; a place you've been to where everything there has been found shimmers (as Monty on the Run's rooms do), and the picked place's name says how many things are left there or ALL FOUND. | Always |
| **Travel back** | None: every step on foot. | On the pause screen, anywhere on the ship: the arrows (or a tap anywhere on a part, hall or cabin) pick any part of a deck, hall or cabin he's stood in (the picked box goes round the whole part: its squares are one place), Enter (or a second tap) takes him there (out of where he is and in at the place's door or end, the way the game itself goes, changing decks as its ladders do; from the middle of a jump, a strike or a climb too); nothing taken comes back. A cabin counts once he has stayed on his feet in it for a moment, so a cabin whose trapdoor drops him on the spot he comes in at (it is the way down) is drawn NO TRAVEL, as are squares he has only passed. | Always |
| **Back to room start** | A trap with no way out means starting again. | B on the pause screen: back to how things were on coming into this room (the latest autosave). | Always |
| **Back to the title** | None (only by losing, or a restart). | T on the pause screen (or a tap on it): the title, with the save kept; ENTER CONTINUE goes back to it, and only a new game started there begins a new one (keeping the save as the previous one). A new game over a save asks first: NEW GAME? (Space or fire again yes, Esc or a tap no, Enter continues); a jump or fire still held as the last life goes brings CONTINUE? up instead of starting one. | Always |
| **Autosave** | None. | Progress is saved in your browser each time Pendragon comes onto a new screen. Launched with a save, the game goes straight back into play where it was (the title only without one, or with T on the pause screen). Starting a new game keeps the save from before it as the previous one: the saved games (F1 on the directory, or the gear there) bring it back, or delete a save (they ask first). | Always |
| **Continue** | When his energy runs out, back to the title. | "CONTINUE?" (Enter yes, Esc no; it waits for an answer): back to the screen he last came onto, with full energy. Not offered after F1 (restart on purpose). | Always |
| **Action keys** | One fire button for the current action; any other key cycles through the actions. | Z jump, X sword (cycling with other keys still works). | Always |
| **Combat effects** | A creature on deck the sword reaches turns grey and floats away; a hurt flashes Pendragon, then takes energy. | Drawn over the picture, never in it, from the moments in the game's own code (re/README.md, "Combat"): an enemy destroyed by the sword flashes white (a pale one yellow), a ring goes out and it bursts into pixels of its own colours, thrown away from the blow; a blow that doesn't destroy sparks; the sword leaves a fading trail; a hurt flashes the picture's edges red, shakes it a pixel or two and pulses the energy left in the status line. Each has a sound of its own, made as a C64 game makes them, on a second SID (the game's own sound untouched). | Always |
| **Running** | Pendragon walks. | Hold Shift to run: his own movement runs twice a frame (enemies, air and timers keep their pace). | When Shift is held |
| **Smooth movement** | Locked to a 50 Hz PAL television; sprites the game multiplexes flicker. | Game logic still runs at the original 50 Hz; sprites glide sideways between frames on 60/120/144 Hz screens, and every sprite shows every frame. Up and down they are drawn where the game puts them, so a figure stands on its floor. | Always |
| **Title screen keys** | The title lists the C64's keys (F7, F1, "any other key", the joystick button). | Rewritten for this keyboard: P to pause, R to restart, Enter to change, Space or fire to start (same layout, the game's own font). | Always |
| **Controls** | Joystick in port 2; F7 pauses, F1 restarts, any other key changes the action. | Arrows/WASD, Space (or Z, X, pad A) for fire, P or Esc for the pause screen, R (or F1) to restart, Enter (or any other key) changes the action; gamepads. M mutes. | Always |
| **Touch controls** (phones, tablets) | None. | 8-way stick, USE (the current action), JUMP, SWORD, RUN (on/off); PAUSE opens the map (tap a place, tap again to go; tap an option to change it); RETURN key, HOME. Landscape with the controls beside the picture (even with rotation locked), or upright. See docs/mobile.md. | On a touch screen |
| **A moment's grace** | None. | For 3 seconds (150 frames) after the game puts you back into play, no hurt starts (the game's own hurt is cancelled where it begins, as SAFE AFTER A HIT does): continuing a save (ENTER CONTINUE, a launch going straight back into play, CONTINUE? after losing), back to room start, arriving from the map. Quality of life for a modern playthrough, so a resume never starts in the middle of a fight; no sign on screen. Both modes. | Always |
| **ENHANCED: safe after a hit** | Each hurt starts as soon as the last one's flash ends: a creature still touching him after a hurt's flash hurts him again. | For 1.5 seconds after a hurt starts, no new one does (the game's own start of it is stopped). | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: knockback** | What touches him stays on him. | When hurt he's pushed away from what hit him: his own walk for 6 frames, three steps a frame (walls stop him as ever; not at the screen's edge). | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: hit-stop** | None. | The game waits 4 frames when an enemy is destroyed (the effects go on), so the blow lands. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: 8-way controls** | The joystick's diagonals are each one of the four ways: up+right goes up, up+left left, down+right right, down+left down. Fire strikes the way he faces, so to strike behind him he walks that way first. | A diagonal moves both ways at once: sideways as ever, and his own step into or out of the picture every other frame (his own pace; walls stop it as they stop him). At a door, stairs or a corner that way he goes straight there, through as walking straight does, then on diagonally. A jump goes the same way. Fire with a direction turns him that way first, then strikes (a diagonal sideways). Keyboard: two arrows (or WASD) at once; touch: the stick's diagonals; pads. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: up a ladder, stay up** | Up takes him up a ladder into the cabin above, where the hole he came through is behind him: Up still held walks him into it and straight back down the ladder, and so on. | Up (or the stick) held as the climb ends is ignored until you let go of it, so he stands in the cabin above. Let go and press again to go back down. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: GUIDED (the story, and sparkles)** | The status line says SEARCHING FOR THE CAPTAINS SOUL, and "YOU HAVE FOUND ..." for the last thing found. Nothing says why a thing is wanted or where one is: the bell is inside a wall, the book in a door, the mirror is yellow lines on yellow sacks, the first piece of the chart a small roll on a shelf, the captain's door only appears once the chart is whole. | Quality of life for a modern playthrough, so the story and what to do are in the picture. Pendragon says what he makes of things, in the first person, on that same status line in the game's own lettering (a long line in parts, each shown long enough to read; never over the game's own "YOU HAVE FOUND ..." message, which comes first): the soul he is here for, the sword and why it matters, what the rite (bell, book and candle) is for and when it is complete, the navigator's tools, the sirens' comb and mirror, each piece of the chart found and how many remain, the shelf, the captain, a trapdoor giving way. Coming into a room with something to find says so once per visit, and not again once it is done. Sparkles (as Entombed's hints) mark what he can take now, exactly where its sprite is, hidden or not, and the guard, the sirens or the captain when it is his move: never on something the game would not give him yet (before its key, sword, tools or rite is in hand) or already taken. On deck 5 he notes the sealed door once, where the captain's cabin will open (the door itself is the game's, drawn when the chart is whole). With nothing to do here and nothing said for about 20 seconds, NEXT: names the next thing he can get and its deck. The game, its rooms and its rules are as they were: only the status line's text and the sparkles are added. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: trapdoors stay open** | A trapdoor that gives way drops him a deck; back in that cabin, the boards are whole again and look like floor. | Once one has given way, it shows as the hole it became each time he comes back, so he can see where it is and go round it (or jump it). It is still a hole: walk onto it and down he goes. Remembered with the saves; a new game starts with them all closed. (Room $21's two cabins have theirs where he comes in, so those still drop him at once.) | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: the ending** | The captain is freed, the game says you have completed it and goes back to the title. | A reward for finishing, after the game's own ending (which is as it was): a montage of places aboard, each the game itself running on a new game of its own with its creatures moving as they do and Pendragon running through it by the joystick, doing one thing that fits (striking the piece off the shelf, the skull hall's imp, taking the pearl, the captain's blow), the place named on the status line; the ship's creatures walk across the rooms Pendragon runs through (places they are never in), their own pictures and frames at their own size, each named for a moment in the black band under the picture as it passes the middle (ours: the game names none), so every one has been seen by the end; with credits over the montage (the game and its makers, facts about it, this port: a line at a time in the same band, between the names; sources in `re/credits.md`), then THE END with the things found, places seen and time, and back to the title (about 70 seconds in all). The game's title tune plays throughout; any key or tap skips it after two seconds. Winning is remembered (apart from the saves, so a new game never loses it): from then on W on the pause screen shows it again, and `?ending` on the page shows it at once. Quality of life for a modern playthrough: a finish worth watching. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: Dragonskulle's figure** | Pendragon already has Dragonskulle's walk, stand and jump, with a grey tunic. | His tunic is Dragonskulle's green (the sword swing is drawn in the same figure already). What touches what is the game's own (a picture only). | ENHANCED |

## Not changed

Everything else is the original: every room, every enemy, the puzzles, the
timings and the sound.

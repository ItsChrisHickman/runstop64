# Starquake: changes from the original

This is the 1985 Commodore 64 game (Nick Strange's conversion of Stephen Crow's Spectrum game) as it was, a native port
from the original's own code and data (checked frame for frame against it; `re/README.md`). **ORIGINAL or ENHANCED:** one
switch, E on the pause screen; ENHANCED is the default and turns on the rows marked ENHANCED, quality-of-life changes for
a modern playthrough. ORIGINAL is the game as it was. Rows marked Always are in both modes. Continuing after a game over
starts with four lives in both.

| Feature | The original | In this remake | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Title** | The loading picture, then a text screen; any key starts. | The same picture, alternating with a page of credits and keys; Space (or fire) starts. The intro ("touch down imminent...") is the original's, without the crack's "cracked by" line. | Always |
| **Pause screen** (P, Esc, pad Start, the gear) | F3 paused. | The shared runstop64 pause screen: a map of the planet (two pages: the surface and the depths), rooms appearing as BLOB visits them, the core and teleports marked. | Always |
| **Travel** | None. | From the map, go back to any room BLOB has been in: he leaves the room he's in as by an exit and arrives where he first stood in that one. | Always |
| **Autosave and Continue** | Game over: back to the start. | Progress is saved in the browser each time BLOB comes into a room. The title offers Continue; after game over, "CONTINUE?" goes back to the last room entered, as it was then. Launched with a save, the game opens on its title with ENTER CONTINUE, and Enter goes back into play where it was (T on the pause screen goes back to the title the same way). Starting a new game keeps the save from before it as the previous one: the saved games (F1 on the directory, or the gear there) bring it back, or delete a save (they ask first). | Always |
| **Core of heroes** (high scores) | Reset every time the game was loaded. | Kept in your browser. | Always |
| **Back to room start** | None. | On the pause screen (B): the room as it was when BLOB came in. | Always |
| **Back to the title** | None (only by losing, or a restart). | T on the pause screen (or a tap on it): the title, with the save kept; ENTER CONTINUE goes back to it, and only a new game started there begins a new one (keeping the save as the previous one). A new game over a save asks first: NEW GAME? (Space or fire again yes, Esc or a tap no, Enter continues); a jump or fire still held as the last life goes brings CONTINUE? up instead of starting one. | Always |
| **Smooth movement** | Locked to a 50 Hz PAL television (and slower when the C64 is busy). | Game logic runs one of the original's frames per 50 Hz tick; sprites glide sideways between ticks on 60/120/144 Hz screens. Up and down they are drawn where the game puts them, so a figure stands on its floor. V switches to whole-pixel steps. | Smooth |
| **Sound** | The music on the title screens, effects in play. | The same, played by the original's own music and effect players (a .sid made from the game). M turns sound off and on. | On |
| **ENHANCED: full energy, platforms and laser** | Energy, platforms and laser charge only from bonuses; each platform and each shot spends some. | BLOB's energy is full each time he comes into a room, and the platforms and the laser never run down (kept full every frame, the bars drawn full), so building up to a core piece and shooting your way through are never held back. Checked by `test/supplies.ts`. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: down lets go of a lift** | A lift tube carries BLOB up while his left foot is in it; down does nothing there. In five rooms (27, 40, 44, 54, 75) a tube floats three cells high with solid walls and open ends: carried up its top, he bobs on the rim with his body against the wall's top cell, and neither left nor right frees him, so the only way out is the energy running out. | Holding down in a lift lets go of it: he drops out of the tube, and that press builds no platform. Up and sideways are as they were. Checked by `test/lifts.ts`. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: a touch destroys an enemy** | An enemy that drifts into BLOB drains a unit of energy every frame it overlaps him, and stays there, so a fight with one is a slow leak; only the laser destroys it. | An enemy BLOB touches is destroyed at once, with the explosion the laser's kill shows (no points: they are for the laser), and takes one fixed bite of energy, a quarter of a full bar (30 of 120), instead of the drain; energy is still full on coming into a room, and a bite never takes more than there is. The enemies that kill outright in the original still do: the homing ones that chase him (they are the ones the laser is for), the plants, and the core's guardians; a moment's grace still protects him. Checked by `test/contact.ts`. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: GET works on the hover pad** | Up picks up, but only on foot: on the hover pad up rises, so nothing can be picked up while flying, and BLOB has to land first (Z is up as well). | Z (the touch GET button presses it) is GET, a key of its own: it picks up what BLOB is at on foot and on the hover pad, where the arrow up still rises. Up on foot still picks up. GET on nothing does nothing (it puts nothing down). On the touch letter screens GET is the stick's up. ORIGINAL's controls are as they were (Z is up). Checked by `test/get.ts`. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: the map marks the pieces** | Nothing says where the core's pieces, the access card and the key lie, or what a door or pyramid asks for. | The map marks (green) the rooms where what the core still wants lies, even rooms BLOB has not been in (where the game put them at the start, or where BLOB put them down), and (pink) the access card and the key; the room line says so, and says a door wants 3 symbols and a pyramid 2, each the same symbol or the access card (the symbols differ each time the room is entered, so the map cannot name them). | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: unlimited lives and continues** | Four lives (more from bonuses); the last one lost is GAME OVER. | A death takes no life: the lives stay as they are, so the game never ends and never asks CONTINUE?. He is still put back as the game puts him, and nothing makes him invincible. For a modern playthrough of a classic; the same in every game. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: the reward for winning** | The core is whole: its picture and the framed message, a key, the final count, game over; nothing more to see. | The game's own ending stays as it was (the core drawn, the message, a key); then the show: a montage of places on the planet (BLOB's own start and the cave below it, a hover pad over the surface and another in a cave, the pieces of the core and the platforms built up to them, teleport booths with a destination's name typed, the core with its last pieces put in and the game's own ending), each one the game's own engine running with its creatures moving and BLOB driven by input like a player's; credits typing on a line at a time in the screen's last text row, which the game never draws in (the makers, true facts, then the port); and the game's creatures walking across the places BLOB runs through (places they are never in), their own sprites and frames, each given a plain name in the same row for a moment as it passes the middle, so every one has been seen by the end; then THE END with the pause screen's numbers (score, rooms, lives) and the title tune all through (about 70 seconds in all); then the final count and game over go on as before. A key or a tap skips it after two seconds. That the game has been won is kept apart from the saves; once won, W on the pause screen shows it again (it ends on the title), and `?ending` on the page shows it at once. Sources of every credit line: `re/credits.md`. Checked by `test/reward.ts` | ENHANCED |
| **What the map says** | The map showed the rooms visited, the core and the teleports, and nothing of what the game wants. | Under the map: the goal, REBUILD THE CORE: n OF 9 (the core's places filled, as the game counts them), and, for the room picked, what it has: the core, a security door, a Cheops pyramid BLOB has met (marked where he met it), a teleport (with its name once BLOB has stood in its booth: the name to type to come to it). Doors and pyramids are marked on the map. The info sheet tells what the game is: carry up to four things to the core, what doors and pyramids ask, what teleports take. Checked by `test/mapinfo.ts`. | Always |
| **Touch answers** | The teleports' names, a hero's initials and the Cheops trade (1-5) are typed. | On a phone the five letters are entered with the touch controls: the stick (up and down) changes the letter under the cursor, FIRE puts it in (the fifth ends it, as the original), left rubs out, RET finishes; a line under the screen says so. The trade's five things take a tap. | Always (phones) |
| **Controls** | Joystick, or Z X F5 F7 and Shift. | Arrows/WASD (up picks up, down builds a platform), Z and X as the same up and down (not while letters are typed), Space (or Ctrl) fires, gamepads, touch (stick, FIRE, a GET button that presses Z and a PAD button that holds down; the stick's still work). In ENHANCED, Z is GET alone (next row). F1 (abort) is the shell's menu. | Always |
| **A moment's grace** | None. | For 3 seconds (150 frames) after the game puts you back into play, nothing kills BLOB or takes his energy (the energy clock waits): continuing a save (ENTER CONTINUE, CONTINUE? after losing), back to room start, arriving from the map. Quality of life for a modern playthrough, so a resume never starts in the middle of a fight; no sign on screen. Both modes. | Always |

## Not changed

Everything else is the original's: all 512 rooms (identical to the C64's,
byte for byte), the items and the core's pieces scattered as the original
scatters them, BLOB's walking, falling, platforms, lifts and hover pads, the
laser, the enemies and how they move and appear, sparks, bonuses, the panel,
teleports (type a destination's name), security doors and Cheops pyramids
(and their trades), key and flat barriers, the core (pieces put back, its
guardians, the end), the final count, the core of heroes (with its quirks:
only five digits of a score count, and the sixth isn't copied), the sounds and
the music.

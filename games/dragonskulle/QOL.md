# Dragonskulle: changes from the original

This is a translated port of the 1986 Commodore 64 game: every routine of the original translated to TypeScript and checked
against the original running on an emulated C64 (`re/README.md`). **ORIGINAL or ENHANCED:** one switch, E on the pause
screen (on the right; tap it on a phone); ENHANCED is the default and turns on the rows marked ENHANCED, quality-of-life
changes for a modern playthrough. ORIGINAL is the game as it was. Rows marked Always are in both modes.

In Dragonskulle ENHANCED is: 8-way controls; safe for a moment after a hit; knockback; hit-stop; the dragons flashing when hit; the shovel taken by walking up to it; hints (sparkles on the starfish, the shovel, the place to dig for the
cloak, the wizards that open the bridges, the dragons and the rock that gives the cloak again, and what is left to do at a
bridge or a dragon on the pause screen); the CLOAK icon draining as the cloak runs out; an ending after the game's own.

| **C64 / HD view** | The C64's picture. | The site's C64/HD switch (the button bottom right, on a phone the C64/HD key beside PAUSE, or H, at any time) is a view setting for every game that has an HD view, remembered, never part of the game's own screens. HD draws the same game as a lit 3D scene built from its own picture (depth worked out from which characters are the sand), his own light in the caves, the sky and the sea dim past the beach. It is laid out as the C64 view is (the status line at the top, the action bar at the bottom), its sprites standing in it as lit billboards with shadows, with bloom, a gentle depth of field and drifting dust, from a camera fixed for the room. Where the browser's graphics can't draw it, the C64 view stays for that visit. The view only: the game, its saves, the map and travel are the same (`re/README.md`, "The HD view"). | C64 |
| **Pause screen and map** | F7 (or the PAUSE icon) paused the game. | P, Esc, pad Start or the gear: the game waits while a pause screen shows a map of the island as the game keeps it (its table of screens, 17 by 24): the screens Pendragon has been on as he finds them, rooms and passages told apart, the long scrolling rows (the beach, the corridors) joined, the ways he's walked between them drawn, his own blinking; with his lives, energy, the shovel and cloak found, and the screens found. | Always |
| **Travel back** | None: every step on foot through a maze of more than a hundred screens. | On the pause screen the arrows (or a tap) pick a screen he has come onto, Enter (or a second tap) takes him there, to where he first came onto it, the way walking off the edge of a screen takes him (the game's own change of screen). Lives, energy, score, what's been found and done stay as they are. | Always |
| **Back to room start** | A trap with no way out means starting again. | B on the pause screen: back to how things were on coming onto this screen (the latest autosave). | Always |
| **Back to the title** | None (only by losing, or a restart). | T on the pause screen (or a tap on it): the title, with the save kept; ENTER CONTINUE goes back to it, and only a new game started there begins a new one (keeping the save as the previous one). A new game over a save asks first: NEW GAME? (Space or fire again yes, Esc or a tap no, Enter continues); a jump or fire still held as the last life goes brings CONTINUE? up instead of starting one. | Always |
| **Autosave** | None. | Progress is saved in your browser each time Pendragon comes onto a new screen. Launched with a save, the game goes straight back into play where it was (the title only without one, or with T on the pause screen). Starting a new game keeps the save from before it as the previous one: the saved games (F1 on the directory, or the gear there) bring it back, or delete a save (they ask first). | Always |
| **Continue** | When the last life (face) goes, back to the title. | "CONTINUE?" (Enter yes, Esc no; it waits for an answer): back to the screen he last came onto, with all five lives. Not offered after R/F1 (restart on purpose). | Always |
| **The action bar** | Five icons along the top of the screen, left to right SHOVEL, CLOAK, PAUSE, STONE, JUMP, unlabelled, with the score and the faces (lives) along the bottom. | The score and faces at the top, the actions along the bottom in the order of their keys, each labelled below, centred, with its key in the game's own letters: Z JUMP, X STONE, C DIG, V CLOAK. To make room the play area is shown four lines higher, without its top four lines (never drawn on in play; sprites there are drawn whole, moved with it). The icons and the hand are the game's own as it draws them (moved, not redrawn), so the hand, and the flash of an icon not yet found, are as ever. No PAUSE icon: P or Esc open the pause screen, which pauses the game as the icon did (the icon waited for fire with the music off and its frame flashing). Only how the picture is shown changes: the game's memory and play are the original's. | Always |
| **Action keys** | One fire button for the icon the hand points at; holding any other key moves the hand on, an icon every 5 frames held (through PAUSE). | Z jump, X stone, C dig, V cloak: the hand straight onto that icon and the action under way in the next frame, as soon as fire with the hand already there. Enter (or any other key) moves the hand one icon along the bar a press (never onto PAUSE). | Always |
| **Energy on the face** | Nothing shows the energy of the life under way (20 points, a hurt takes some): a face just turns into a skull when it's all gone. | The face of the life under way (the leftmost not yet a skull) empties from the top to dark grey as the energy goes, a line at a time (21 lines for 20 points), as a C64 game might have drawn it; full energy is the face as the game draws it, and when it's gone the game's own skull is there as ever. Only how the picture is shown: the game's memory and play are the original's. | Always |
| **Combat effects** | A stone that hits a creature: it goes, or (a dragon) takes the hit; a hurt flashes Pendragon, then takes energy from the face going. | Drawn over the picture, never in it, from the moments in the game's own code (re/README.md, "Combat"): an enemy destroyed by a stone flashes white, a ring goes out and it bursts into pixels of its own colours; a blow that doesn't destroy sparks; the stone leaves a fading trail; a hurt flashes the picture's edges red, shakes it a pixel or two and pulses the faces in the status line. Each has a sound of its own, on a second SID (the game's own sound untouched). | Always |
| **Running** | Pendragon walks. | Hold Shift to run: his own movement runs twice a frame (creatures and timers keep their pace). | When Shift is held |
| **Smooth movement** | Locked to a 50 Hz PAL television. | Game logic still runs at the original 50 Hz; sprites glide between frames on 60/120/144 Hz screens. | Always |
| **Title screen keys** | The title lists the C64's keys (F7, F1, "any other key", the joystick button). | Rewritten for this keyboard: P to pause, R to restart, Space or fire to start (same layout, the game's own font). The action keys aren't listed (the bar labels them in play): their line is left empty, and ENTER CONTINUE goes there when there's a save. The copyright line (a cracker's year in the only copy that runs) is the original's again. | Always |
| **Controls** | Joystick in port 2; F7 pauses, F1 restarts, any other key moves the hand. | Arrows/WASD, Space (or pad A) for fire, Z/X/C/V straight to an action, P or Esc for the pause screen, R (or F1) to restart, Enter (or any other key) moves the hand along the bar; gamepads. M mutes. | Always |
| **Touch controls** (phones, tablets) | None. | 8-way stick, USE (the icon the hand points at), JUMP, STONE, DIG, CLOAK, RUN (on/off); PAUSE opens the map (tap a place, tap again to go; tap E to switch ORIGINAL/ENHANCED); RETURN key, HOME. Landscape with the controls beside the picture (even with rotation locked), or upright. See docs/mobile.md. | On a touch screen |
| **A moment's grace** | None. | For 3 seconds (150 frames) after the game puts you back into play, no hurt starts (the game's own hurt is cancelled where it begins, as SAFE AFTER A HIT does): continuing a save (ENTER CONTINUE, a launch going straight back into play, CONTINUE? after losing), back to room start, arriving from the map. Quality of life for a modern playthrough, so a resume never starts in the middle of a fight; no sign on screen. Both modes. | Always |
| **ENHANCED: safe after a hit** | Each hurt starts as soon as the last one's flash ends: a creature (or the gas) still on him hurts him again. | For 1.5 seconds after a hurt starts, no new one does (the game's own start of it is stopped). | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: knockback** | What touches him stays on him. | When hurt he's pushed away from what hit him: his own walk for a few frames (walls stop him as ever; not at the screen's edge). | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: hit-stop** | None. | The game waits 4 frames when an enemy is destroyed (the effects go on), so the blow lands. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: the dragon shows its hits** | A stone that counts on a dragon (ten are needed, thrown facing up) changes nothing on it: nothing says which stones count; only its death, at the tenth, cycles its colours. | Each stone the game counts on a dragon (at the game's own `inc $676F`) flashes the dragon through white, yellow and light red for 8 frames (a colour cycle, as the C64 does with palette changes), with the hit's spark and sound as for any blow; stones that don't count (thrown sideways) don't. The tenth flashes it too, then the game's own death goes on. Only how the picture is shown (the frame's sprites): the game's colour registers and memory are the original's, so nothing is left changed in a save. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: 8-way controls** | The joystick's diagonals are each one of the four ways: up+right goes up, up+left left, down+right right, down+left down. Fire uses the way he faces, so to throw behind him he walks that way first. | A diagonal moves both ways at once: sideways as ever, and his own step into or out of the picture every other frame (his own pace; walls stop it as they stop him). At a way up or down out of a screen he goes straight there, through as walking straight does. A jump goes the same way. Fire with a direction turns him that way first, then throws. Keyboard: two arrows (or WASD) at once; touch: the stick's diagonals; pads. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: the shovel by hand** | The shovel lies at the back of its room between two ant-like guards, and only a stone takes it: thrown facing up, from below and to its left (it flies up and to the right and must meet the shovel within 8 pixels across, high enough), within about 9 seconds of coming in, before the ants close in round it (then no stone takes it until he leaves and comes back). Walking up to it, even standing on it, does nothing, and nothing says why. | Walking up to it takes it: when he touches it, the game's own taking of it runs (the code its stone check runs: the shovel gone, the SHOVEL icon his, its flash, sound and score), at the room's own check of the collisions each frame. The ants close in as ever, but the shovel can be taken by hand whenever he reaches it (an ant on him at the back still hurts him, as ever). A stone takes it too, as in the original. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: hints** | None: a solution sheet or a lot of trying. The game never says what opens a bridge, what a dragon wants, or where the cloak comes from a second time (`re/README.md`, "Rules the game never explains"). | Sparkles on what moves the game on, until it's done: the starfish that stops the skull's rays at the caves' mouth, the shovel (walking up to it takes it), and, with the shovel, the place on the beach to dig up the cloak; on a river screen the wizard on the far bank (a stone on him counts one towards the bridge: 10, 15 or 20 for the island's three, shut until then) until the bridge is open; on a dragon's head (ten stones thrown facing up) until it is dead; and, once the cloak has been dug up and none is in hand, the flickering rock that falls in the screens of the falling rocks (catching it gives the cloak again, as often as it falls). On the pause screen, what is still to do where he stands (BRIDGE / KILL 7 MORE, DRAGON / 6 HITS LEFT) and the seconds the cloak has left. Read from the game's code and checked by playing, on the translation and on the original (`re/README.md`, "Hints"; `re/rules/check.ts`, `re/hints/check.ts`, `test/rules.ts`). | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: the ending** | The skull's last hit, the explosion, and a screen that says you have completed the series (signed DT and RET 1985); then the title. | A reward for finishing, after the game's own ending (which is as it was): a montage of places on the island, each the game itself running on a new game of its own with its creatures moving as they do and Pendragon running through it by the joystick, doing one thing that fits (digging the cloak up on the beach, taking the shovel with a stone, crossing the bridge, stones at the dragon, digging a grave, the cloak on under the falling rocks and in the gas, stones at the eyes and at the skull), the place named on the status panel at the top; then a parade of the island's creatures, their own pictures and frames walking across one at a time at double size with a name under each (ours: the game names none), Pendragon last; with credits over the montage (the game and its makers, facts about it, this port: a line at a time in the empty band of the status panel under the caption; sources in `re/credits.md`), then THE END with the score, lives left, dragons slain, bridges opened and screens seen, and back to the title. The game's title tune plays throughout; any key or tap skips it after two seconds. Winning is remembered (apart from the saves, so a new game never loses it): from then on W on the pause screen shows it again, and `?ending` on the page shows it at once. Quality of life for a modern playthrough: a finish worth watching. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: the cloak running out** | The cloak lasts 769 frames (15.3 seconds) and ends without a sign: the colour of Pendragon stops flickering, and the icon goes. | The CLOAK icon (V) drains from the top, to dark grey, a line at a time as its frames count down, as the energy drains the face; full again when it ends. Only how the picture is shown: the game's memory and play are the original's. | ENHANCED |

## Not changed

Everything else is the original: every screen, every creature, the puzzles, the
timings and the sound. The bar's new place and order are how the picture is shown, not
the game: its memory, collisions and timing are the original's.

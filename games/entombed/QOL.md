# Entombed: changes from the original

This is a translated port of the 1985 Commodore 64 game: every routine of the original translated to TypeScript and checked
against the original running on an emulated C64 (`re/README.md`). **ORIGINAL or ENHANCED:** one switch, E on the pause
screen (on the right; tap it on a phone); ENHANCED is the default and turns on the rows marked ENHANCED, quality-of-life
changes for a modern playthrough. ORIGINAL is the game as it was. Rows marked Always are in both modes.

| Feature | The original | In this port | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pause screen and map** | F7 paused the game. | P, Esc, pad Start or the gear: the game waits while a pause screen shows a map of the whole tomb, headed with the floor he's on (FLOOR 6 OF 7): all seven floors at once, one scale, each by its number (his in yellow, one he's not been on dark), their corridors from the game's own maze tables (only the rows and columns each floor's tables hold, with dead ends drawn as stubs), dark until visited; junctions as squares, chambers beside their doors and stairs (an arrow in place of the junction) once found; Pendragon's place blinking, moving with him along the corridors; a key to the marks, and how many of the tomb's junctions he's seen), energy and progress, and the options (O: a page of every option and assist with its key, to tap or press). | Always |
| **Travel back** | None: every step on foot. | On the pause screen: the arrows pick any junction he's been to, on any floor (the box just round it), Enter takes him there (out of a chamber too, leaving it as the game would). | Always |
| **Back to room start** | A trap with no way out means starting again. | B on the pause screen: back to how things were on coming into this room (the latest autosave). | Always |
| **Back to the title** | None (only by losing, or a restart). | T on the pause screen (or a tap on it): the title, with the save kept; ENTER CONTINUE goes back to it, and only a new game started there begins a new one (keeping the save as the previous one). A new game over a save asks first: NEW GAME? (Space or fire again yes, Esc or a tap no, Enter continues); a jump or fire still held as the last life goes brings CONTINUE? up instead of starting one. | Always |
| **Autosave** | None. | Progress is saved in your browser each time Pendragon comes onto a new screen. Launched with a save, the game goes straight back into play where it was (the title only without one, or with T on the pause screen). Starting a new game keeps the save from before it as the previous one: the saved games (F1 on the directory, or the gear there) bring it back, or delete a save (they ask first). | Always |
| **Continue** | When the air runs out or the last ankh fades, back to the title. | "CONTINUE?" (Enter yes, Esc no; it waits for an answer): back to the screen he last came onto, with full health and air. Not offered after F1 (restart on purpose). | Always |
| **Action keys** | One fire button for the current action; any other key cycles through the actions. | Z jump, X whip, C torch (cycling with other keys still works). | Always |
| **Combat effects** | The whip makes a corridor monster just vanish; a hurt flashes Pendragon (and a rising note) for 16 frames, then an ankh fades a step. | Drawn over the picture, never in it, from the moments in the game's own code (re/README.md, "Combat"): an enemy destroyed by the whip flashes white (a pale one yellow), a ring goes out and it bursts into pixels of its own colours, thrown away from the blow; a blow that doesn't destroy sparks; the whip leaves a fading trail; a hurt flashes the picture's edges red, shakes it a pixel or two and pulses the ankhs in the status line. Each has a sound of its own, made as a C64 game makes them, on a second SID (the game's own sound untouched). | Always |
| **Running** | Pendragon walks. | Hold Shift to run: his own movement runs twice a frame (enemies, air and timers keep their pace). | When Shift is held |
| **Smooth movement** | Locked to a 50 Hz PAL television; sprites the game multiplexes flicker. | Game logic still runs at the original 50 Hz; sprites glide sideways between frames on 60/120/144 Hz screens, and every sprite shows every frame. Up and down they are drawn where the game puts them, so a figure stands on its floor. | Always |
| **Title screen keys** | The title lists the C64's keys (F7, F1, "any other key", the joystick button). | Rewritten for this keyboard: P to pause, R to restart, Enter to change, Space or fire to start (same layout, the game's own font). | Always |
| **Controls** | Joystick in port 2; F7 pauses, F1 restarts, any other key changes the action. | Arrows/WASD, Space (or Z, X, pad A) for fire, P or Esc for the pause screen, R (or F1) to restart, Enter (or any other key) changes the action; gamepads. M mutes. | Always |
| **Touch controls** (phones, tablets) | None. | 8-way stick, USE (the current action), JUMP, WHIP, TORCH, RUN (on/off); PAUSE opens the map (tap a place, tap again to go; tap an option to change it); RETURN key, HOME. Landscape with the controls beside the picture (even with rotation locked), or upright. See docs/mobile.md. | On a touch screen |
| **A moment's grace** | None. | For 3 seconds (150 frames) after the game puts you back into play, no hurt starts (the game's own hurt is cancelled where it begins, as SAFE AFTER A HIT does): continuing a save (ENTER CONTINUE, a launch going straight back into play, CONTINUE? after losing), back to room start, arriving from the map. Quality of life for a modern playthrough, so a resume never starts in the middle of a fight; no sign on screen. Both modes. | Always |
| **ENHANCED: hints** | Some chambers' puzzles give no clue. | Read from each chamber's code and checked by playing every one automatically (`re/hints/`): 20 of the 25 chambers have them. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: safe after a hit** | Each hurt starts as soon as the last one's flash ends: after a hurt's 16-frame flash a monster still touching him hurts him again at once. | For 1.5 seconds after a hurt starts, no new one does (the game's own start of it is stopped). | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: knockback** | What touches him stays on him. | When hurt he's pushed away from what hit him: his own walk for 6 frames, three steps a frame (walls stop him as ever; not at the screen's edge). | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: hit-stop** | None. | The game waits 4 frames when an enemy is destroyed (the effects go on), so the blow lands. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: 8-way controls** | The joystick's diagonals are each one of the four ways: up+right goes up, up+left left, down+right right, down+left down. Fire strikes the way he faces, so to strike behind him he walks that way first. | A diagonal moves both ways at once: sideways as ever, and his own step into or out of the picture every other frame (his own pace; walls stop it as they stop him). At a door, stairs or a corner that way he goes straight there, through as walking straight does, then on diagonally. A jump goes the same way. Fire with a direction turns him that way first, then strikes (a diagonal sideways). Keyboard: two arrows (or WASD) at once; touch: the stick's diagonals; pads. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: the ending** | He walks out by the way out and the game says congratulations you have completed it, then goes back to the title. | A reward for finishing, after the game's own ending (which is as it was): a montage of eight places in the tomb, each the game itself running on a new game of its own with its creatures moving as they do and Pendragon running through it by the joystick doing what fits the place (the five bells whipped in order, the statue, the bowl of fruit and the book, the cobra, the leap up the bird's chamber, the way out run for), the place named on the game's message bar; credits over it in the way of Super Mario World's (the game and its makers, true facts about it, this port: a line at a time in the same band, between the names; sources in `re/credits.md`); the tomb's creatures walk across the chambers Pendragon runs through (places they are never in), their own pictures and frames at their own size, each named for a moment in the black band under the picture as it passes the middle (plain names: the game names none), so every one has been seen by the end; THE END with the chambers entered, junctions seen, air left and time; and back to the title (about 70 seconds in all). The game has no music, so what is heard is the places' own sound (the whip, the bells). Any key or tap skips it after two seconds. Winning is remembered (apart from the saves, so a new game never loses it): from then on W on the pause screen shows it again, and `?ending` on the page shows it at once. Quality of life for a modern playthrough: a finish worth watching. | ENHANCED |
| **ENHANCED: Dragonskulle's figure** | Pendragon is the figure in the cap and waistcoat. | Pendragon is drawn as he is in Dragonskulle (his face, green tunic and black cape), in every pose: standing, each walk step, jumping and the whip (the arm out). His feet stand where the old figure's did, and what touches what is the game's own (a picture only). The ghost that rises when he dies is unchanged; in a dark chamber his tunic goes dark as before. | ENHANCED |

## Not changed

Everything else is the original: every room, every enemy, the puzzles, the
timings and the sound.

The stairs too: the gates in chambers 5 (a stone), 15 (an altar) and 17 (a statue) are shut by
what stands in front of them until the chamber's puzzle is done (jump on the stone 41 times,
carry in the small idol, whip the statue away), as in the original; the other stairs are free
(re/README.md, "The stairs").

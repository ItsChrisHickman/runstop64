# Moley Christmas: the plan

A native rebuild of the 1987 Your Sinclair cover game (ZX Spectrum only), made the way Auf Wiedersehen Monty was: data
extracted from the original, behaviour rebuilt in TypeScript, everything checked against the original running. What the
original is and where its parts live: `games/moley/re/README.md`. Stage 1 (done) is the base: the tape, a headless
Spectrum (`tools/zx`), the notes, this plan. Stages 1 (data), 2 (Monty alone), 3 (the whole game's logic) and 4 (rooms and picture) below are done. The game is small: six screens, a Christmas card, 14 items, 5 lives.

## Stages

1. **Data (done).** `re/extract.ts` writes `src/data/*.ts` (committed, hex strings; `src/data.ts` types them): maps and attributes, both tile sets, 192 sprite frames, gauge images, panel, credits picture, texts, the 66 ROM glyphs the game prints, the jump tables and the five beeper effects as toggle gaps. `test/data.ts` proves it (regenerated identical; equal to the original's memory in every state; glyphs = what the character routine draws; every sprite frame drawn by the game's own routine equals `src/sprite.ts`). Original wording: Extract to `src/data/` (TypeScript, generated by `re/` scripts): the seven tile maps and attribute tables, the two
   tile sets, the 192 sprite frames, the credits picture and HUD panel, the texts, the glyphs the game prints, the beeper
   effects. The rooms are already proved byte for byte (`test/rooms.ts`, `test/title.ts`); sprites get the same check.
2. **Monty alone, in lockstep (done).** `src/monty.ts`, `gauge.ts`, `input.ts`, `cycle.ts` (then in `player.ts`, now `game.ts`); `test/lockstep.ts` compared every pass with the original: slow set 361,600 passes (120,000 random players in rooms 0-5 with the keyboard, Kempston and Sinclair mixed, 241,600 in 2,416 placements round all 302 tile kinds of the six rooms, in walking, jumping, falling, posed and hidden states, some with a nearly empty gauge), **0 differences** in x, y, frame, flags, state, phase, room, lives, gauge, jump keys, ropes, walking cycle, pass counter, the scripts' animation and the keys read; the fast set runs about 15,000. The room scripts' changes to Monty between passes are counted, not compared (758 deaths and 103 room changes seen). Monty's logic uses no R. Original wording: One logic pass is 2 frames (25 a second). Rebuild input, walking, the 24-step jump table,
   falling, ropes, room 5's flying, death and the gauge, and compare with the original every pass (`$822F`: x, y, frame, flags,
   state, phase, room, lives, gauge): random players in every room, then each kind of tile. Lockstep runs on our Z80 model.
3. **Room scripts, enemies, items (done).** `src/game.ts` is the game's logic: `Game.pass(keys)` is the original's main loop, with the six room scripts (`src/rooms/`) over the
   shared primitives (`src/primitives.ts`: the touch test, kills and drains, the two walkers, the bobber), the items (`src/items.ts`: 35 points, the count, the exit that kills
   while items are left), room 2's pistons built by rewriting the map, room 5's carry-and-deliver, deaths and exits as the original does them (the pass is abandoned and the room's
   restart routine runs, then the new room's script in the same pass), lives, score and high score. The game's random numbers are the Z80's R register, read in two places: the
   rebuilt game asks a `Random` (`src/random.ts`) for a byte; the shipped game will use `SeededRandom`, the lockstep test feeds the bytes the original read. The state is
   plain numbers (`snapshot()`, `restore()`: a save, and what the test's bots try moves on). `test/lockstep.ts` compares **everything the game keeps every pass**: Monty, the whole
   entity table, score and high score, lives, gauge, the items and their count, the room's map (room 2 rewrites it) and attributes, every script variable, the pocket picture, the
   beeper effects asked for, the outcome and the random bytes used. Slow set: **355,291 passes, 0 differences** (48,927 random players in rooms 0-5; 85,216 with the scripts' state
   shaken so the later parts of each script are met; 27,868 at the exits; 193,280 in 2,416 placements round all 302 tile kinds), 1,636 deaths, 213 room changes, 10 endings, 53 game
   overs, every way out taken; the fast set about 26,000. **Played to completion in lockstep** (`test/playthrough.ts`, a bot on the original, the rebuilt game compared every pass):
   room 2 from its start to the prize and room 3 (926 passes), room 5 from its start through four deliveries to the ending (606), and the whole game from room 0 to the ending
   (3,963 passes, score 3490, the most there is), all 0 differences; `test/newgame.ts`: a new game from the credits page, and a second game after the first, equal to the original's.
   **One difference, with its root cause** (`test/stray.ts`, `re/README.md`): a kill by the prize's second half in room 2 early in a puzzle round makes the original jump to the wrong
   address (not the room's restart), so Monty is not sent back and loses a life a pass; the rebuild restarts the room (Chris decided 2026-10-17: fixed in both modes, ORIGINAL included). Not reached by play:
   an entity at x `$F0` or more on the bottom rows overruns the original's redraw table.
4. **Rooms and picture (done).** `src/display.ts` is the Spectrum's screen memory (bitmap in the ULA's layout, attributes) changed by the original's own steps: entering a room, the per-pass redraw with the counters (a covered cell is drawn from the map into a copy, the sprites into the copy, then copied; a cell covered last pass is drawn from the map), the room scripts' forced redraws (conveyor, pistons), the conveyor tiles turning, the HUD (faces, dial, item box, score), the death and time-out pictures (`takeShots`). `Game` tells it what happens through an `Observer` (the logic is untouched). `src/look.ts` makes the two looks, `src/view.ts` puts them on c64kit's renderer.
   **Checked against the original** (`test/picture.ts`, slow set, 133,016 passes: random players, shaken states, exits, Monty round every tile kind, the bot in rooms 2 and 5 and the whole game): **0 passes differ** (the whole 6912 bytes of screen memory equal after every pass), and of the 305,130 frames the original showed **0 are different**: 132,771 are the pass's new picture, 59,469 the one before (the draw had not landed in that frame), 73,605 torn between the two (the original draws in place), 39,285 are in a pass that entered a room or lost a life (compared apart: all 570 deaths and time-outs show the original's pictures in order, Monty's three at 2 frames each, the empty one 25, the dial's 56 steps; entering a room the original draws over about 7 frames, the rebuild at once). The fast set runs about 12,000 passes. **Root causes found on the way:** the room scripts force cells to be drawn again (`SET 0` on the redraw counters: room 1's conveyor, room 2's deck and pistons); the tile set has 250 tiles, not 244 (the pistons); the gauge is in cell row 20; a pass in which the ending or game over comes is not drawn. **Not proved:** the ending, game over and credits pictures (stage 5); a shaken entity on frames `$80`-`$88` (the original draws its interrupt table; skipped, 9 passes); an entity whose cells run off the 18 rows (the original's counter table overruns); the browser itself (`view.ts` runs on the headless renderer stand-in, no GPU here).
   **Palette:** `Palette`, `makePalette` and `C64_COLOURS` in c64kit; `Surface.palette` and `PixelRenderer.palette` default to the C64's, so every other game is unchanged (`packages/c64kit/test/palette.test.ts`: the RGBA of a default surface equals the old conversion; the whole fast set passes). The effects layer and the HD layer still read the C64 palette (no Spectrum game uses them).
   **ENHANCED look (a flag, `MoleyView.look`, for the front end to switch later):** no colour clash. The scenery is drawn without the sprites, each sprite whole over it (not hidden behind ink) in one colour, and the sprites glide between the 25 Hz passes (`ViewStep`, like AWM). The colour rule is "the colour the original usually shows it in", measured (`re/colours.ts`): 94 % of the pixels a sprite adds to the screen are bright white and white is the dominant colour of all 81 pictures seen in play, so every sprite is bright white (`SPRITE_INK`); the clash only shows where a sprite crosses a coloured cell. ORIGINAL is the exact picture. `re/frames.ts` writes PNGs of both looks beside the original's screen.
5. **Title, game over, ending, flow.** The credits page and its scroller, GAME OVER, the Christmas card and its scroller,
   the restart, the high score. Compared frame by frame.
6. **Front end.** The shared one (`runPort`, `FrontEnd`): pause screen with a map of the six screens and progress (items taken,
   screens done), travel to any screen reached, autosave per screen, continue, back to screen start, touch controls (stick:
   left, right, up, down; one jump button), info sheet, credits. ORIGINAL / ENHANCED on the pause screen.
7. **Sound.** Beeper effects first (below); the 128K music last, only if wanted.

## The picture

*Stage 4 (done), from Chris:* the Spectrum palette is the default and the clash is the original's; one looks-only ENHANCED item (no colour clash, below); both modes keep the Spectrum's own colours. Built as described in stage 4 above. The room 2 bug (the original's wrong-address jump) is fixed in both modes.

Sprites are drawn the original's way (found in stage 1): into a copy of the screen bitmap, `buffer OR (b AND mask(buffer))`, so a sprite shows only where the background has no ink beside it (`src/sprite.ts`, proved), then each cell is copied to the screen; the two conveyor tiles ($55, $7B) turn one row each pass.

The Spectrum screen is 256 x 192 with 8 x 8 attribute cells (one ink and one paper per cell, 8 colours, bright); sprites take
the colour of the cell they sit in. Our renderer is a 320 x 200 indexed surface with 16 colours and a border.
**Recommend:** (1) compose each frame into a 256 x 192 index buffer as the game does (tiles with their cell attributes, then
the sprites, each sprite pixel coloured by the attribute of the cell under it, so the colour clash is the original's), and
put it in the middle of the 320 x 200 surface (32 pixels left and right, 4 above and below), the border colour around it;
square pixels, no stretching. (2) Use the Spectrum's own palette: it has exactly 16 entries (8 normal, 8 bright), so
the surface needs no new pixel format, only a **palette on the surface and renderer** (default C64). Five places in
c64kit read the C64 palette directly (`surface.ts`, `renderer.ts`, `effects.ts`, the HD layer; no HD view for this game) and would take it
from the renderer; that change helps any later non-C64 game. (3) Mapping Spectrum colours onto C64 ones (or softening them) was tried with `re/looks.ts` and dropped: Chris
found it dull, and the Spectrum's own colours are the real ones. The pause screen and map are Spectrum-style (Spectrum palette, a Spectrum-like
8 x 8 font drawn by us); the shell's overlay stays as it is. Sprites are drawn into the buffer, not as separate quads, so
the cell colours work; ENHANCED's smoothing (below) draws the buffer at every display frame with sprites at in-between
positions.

## Sound

The 48K game has **no music**, only blocking beeper effects (seven routines, five of them called); two of those use bytes of the 48K ROM as noise.
**Recommend:** record each effect on our model as the list of gaps between speaker toggles (about 1300 numbers for the five used,
`re/sounds.ts` already measures them), commit that as data, and at start-up turn each into an `AudioBuffer` by integrating the
1-bit signal over each output sample (a box filter; some half-periods are 44 T-states, far above the sample rate). That is the
original's sound exactly, with no ROM and no Z80 in the browser. The original also has AY music for 128K machines (Benn Daglish; one
tune of about 6 minutes; a 25 Hz player the tape carries at `$E000`). **Recommend** adding it last: the model already has a 128K
mode with AY port logging (`re/music.ts` runs the player and keeps the register stream: 9217 steps of registers 0-10, no
envelope); keep that as data (delta-coded, a few tens of KB) and play it through a small
AY synthesiser (3 tone channels, noise, envelope; about 200 lines, new to c64kit), on in both modes with the M key to mute.

## ENHANCED (few, for a modern playthrough)

1. **Smooth motion:** the display runs at the screen's rate with Monty and the sprites interpolated between the 25 Hz passes
   (AWM does this); the logic is untouched.
2. **Items left:** a count in the HUD pocket box, because touching an exit while items are left costs a life and the original gives no sign.
3. **Steer in the air:** left and right keep working during a jump (the original fixes the direction at take-off).

4. **No colour clash (built, behind `MoleyView.look`):** sprites keep their own colour (bright white, measured) and are drawn whole over the cells like C64 sprites (ORIGINAL keeps the clash). A look only. Smooth motion (item 1) is built with it.

Decide after playing the rebuild; none of these changes a room.

5. ~~C64-style palette~~: tried and dropped 2026-10-17 (it made the game dull; the real colours stay in both modes).

## Risks

- **The R register** drives the random events (room 3's cars, room 5's traffic, piston 1's wait, item colours); lockstep feeds it in (proved), and the
  shipped game's traffic will differ from the original's in detail, not in kind.
- **Room 5 and room 2** were the least understood; both are now played to their ends in lockstep (stage 3).
- **The ROM font**: the game prints digits and "GAME OVER !!!" with the ROM's character set. We commit only the glyphs the game
  prints, extracted like the rest of the original's graphics, and draw our other text in our own font.
- **Timing**: the original's pass takes 2 frames (3 or 4 when a room is entered); the rebuild runs exactly 25 passes a second. Compare allowing a frame either way.
- **No Z80 in the browser**: the model stays a test tool (`tools/zx`). If AY music or anything else later wants it live, move
  the CPU to a package beside c64kit, as it is already a standalone module.
- The model has no memory contention and no floating bus; this game needs neither (it ran identically in all checks), a
  future Spectrum game may.

## Size

About 3000 lines of game code (AWM's is 4500 for 80 rooms), 1500 of tests, a few hundred of data scripts, and
under 100 KB of data (maps and attributes 8.5 KB, tile sets 4 KB, sprites 6 KB, picture 3.4 KB, panel 2.3 KB, effects 8 KB, and the music's register stream, a few tens of KB). Stages 1-5
are the bulk; the front end and sound reuse shared modules.
